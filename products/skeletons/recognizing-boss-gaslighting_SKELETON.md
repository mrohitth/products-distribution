# Product Skeleton: Is My Boss Gaslighting Me?

**Audience Core:** Employees in late-stage questioning — they've already experienced something, replayed it, and wondered if it counts. They don't need education on gaslighting as a concept. They need confirmation and a map.

---

## 1. Product Title

**Is My Boss Gaslighting Me?** *(22 characters)*

*Why this works:* This is the exact query your audience is already typing into Google at 11pm. It's not a clever concept — it's a personal question. Curiosity without false mystery. The specificity signals: this product knows exactly what you're dealing with.

---

## 2. Emotional Hook

You noticed it. The conversation where your idea became theirs. The meeting that didn't happen even though it happened. The way your manager says "we talked about this" when you both know you didn't. You've replayed it enough times to feel like you're going crazy, and you've also thought: maybe I'm just being sensitive.

You're not being sensitive.

This is a field guide for people who keep second-guessing themselves at work. Not because they're fragile — because they're dealing with someone who's designed a system to make them doubt what they know to be true. You'll learn to name the specific behaviors happening to you right now, document them without spiraling, and execute responses that stop the cycle or get you out of it.

Gaslighting at work is rarely one incident. It's a pattern. Once you see it, you can't unsee it — and that's the point. This guide gives you the map before you need it.

---

## 3. Product Architecture

### Part 1: The Behavior Index *(Know It When You See It)*

**Chapter 1 — Identify the Credit Redistribution Pattern**
*Output: You'll be able to name 5 specific scenarios where your boss claimed your work as theirs, and articulate exactly why each one was a theft — not just "a thing that happened."*

The quote in your trend data — "it's ok to give them the credit" — is a masterclass in this pattern. You'll learn the difference between a boss who forgot attribution and one who systematically erases your contributions, including the scripting they use to make you complicit in your own erasure.

**Chapter 2 — Spot the Standard Shift Before It Destroys You**
*Output: You'll map 3 current double-bind situations where your boss has set mutually exclusive expectations and can identify which standard applies when.*

This is the "I told you to be more proactive / I didn't authorize that" trap. You'll build a personal double-bind inventory so you stop accepting blame for rules that change based on convenience.

**Chapter 3 — Recognize the Reality Redefinition Moments**
*Output: You'll identify 4 specific phrases that indicate your boss is rewriting history and a quick-log format to capture these incidents with timestamp, witnesses, and exact language — before you're told it didn't happen.*

The goal: evidence that exists outside your head, before your memory gets reframed.

---

### Part 2: The Documentation Protocol *(Protect Your Reality)*

**Chapter 4 — Build a Parallel Record That Holds Up**
*Output: You'll create a 3-tier documentation system: daily micro-logs (2 minutes), weekly pattern summaries (10 minutes), and incident-specific deep records (for the major ones).*

This is not journaling. It's legal-grade observation. You'll learn what to capture (exact words, who was present, what happened immediately after) and what not to waste time documenting (your feelings about it in the moment). Feelings go in Part 3.

**Chapter 5 — Identify the Gaslighter's Erasure Timeline**
*Output: You'll map your boss's typical cycle from incident → reframing → consequence, so you can anticipate when they'll pretend something didn't happen and have your record ready.*

Most gaslighters follow a predictable arc. Once you see the timeline, you stop being ambushed by it.

**Chapter 6 — Find Your Witness Chain**
*Output: You'll identify which colleagues have seen the same patterns and how to build informal corroboration without becoming a gossip or a target.*

You don't need a conspiracy. You need one or two people who've seen what you've seen — and who can back you in a conversation without it being coordinated.

---

### Part 3: The Response Playbook *(Respond From Clarity, Not Confusion)*

**Chapter 7 — The Exact Phrases That Stop the Reframe in Real Time**
*Output: You'll have 6 word-for-word responses that interrupt a gaslighting moment without escalating — including how to say "I'm noting this happened differently than you described" without being defensive.*

These are not confrontation scripts. They're reality-anchoring tools. The goal is to mark the moment, not win the argument.

**Chapter 8 — Handle the "I'm Concerned About Your Performance" Ambush**
*Output: You'll have a step-by-step protocol for when your boss pivots from manipulation to formal critique, including how to ask "can you show me where this is documented" and what to do when they can't.*

This is the escalation point where most people lose. You'll know how to hold ground.

**Chapter 9 — Decide Whether to Escalate or Exit — and How to Do Both Cleanly**
*Output: You'll use a structured decision framework that evaluates leverage, timeline, industry risk, and personal cost — and generates a specific action plan for either path.*

This is where most guides punt. This product doesn't. You'll know exactly what you're fighting for and exactly what it's costing you.

---

### Part 4: The Recovery Layer *(Your Reality Is Yours Again)*

**Chapter 10 — Rebuild the Calibration You've Been Losing**
*Output: You'll identify 3 specific confidence distortions that gaslighting creates — and run a 30-day re-calibration protocol to trust your own observations again.*

Gaslighting doesn't just hurt while it's happening. It teaches you to distrust yourself permanently unless you actively undo that learning.

**Chapter 11 — Prevent the Next One Before It Starts**
*Output: You'll have a manager audit checklist for any new role that identifies the top 5 warning signs specific to your industry and role level — so you're not caught off guard again.*

The goal isn't cynicism. It's pattern recognition you can turn off when it's not relevant.

**Chapter 12 — Decide What to Tell People — and What Not To**
*Output: You'll have a communication framework for discussing what happened with future employers, colleagues, or mentors without underselling the experience or overselling it as trauma.*

You don't have to carry this silently. You also don't have to make it your whole identity.

## 4. Key Human Touch Elements

**The "I Can't Believe This Is My Life" Entry Point**
The product should open with an acknowledgment that feels like a confession: *You probably didn't expect to be reading a guide about your boss.* This normalizes the experience without dramatizing it.

**The Credit Redistribution Quote Analysis**
The verbatim quote ("it's ok to give them the credit") should appear early and function as the primary case study — because it's so normalized that readers often don't recognize it as manipulation until it's explicitly named. This is the hook that converts browsers to buyers.

**The "You're Not Broken" Closing Beat**
The final section must explicitly address the identity damage — that people who've been gaslit often carry shame about not catching it sooner or not "doing something about it." This is where the product earns trust: by naming the thing they can't say out loud.

**The "This Happened Before" Pattern**
Throughout the guide, highlight that gaslighting tactics are recycled across industries and boss archetypes. This provides relief (not unique, not your fault) without minimizing the specific pain.

## 5. Format & Pricing

**Format:** 12-chapter PDF (estimated 40-50 pages) + 3-page Quick Reference Card (laminated, designed to live at your desk) + digital checklist for documenting incidents in real time

**Price Point Rationale:** $47–$67

*Rationale:* This is a pain point with extremely high emotional stakes and no obvious free resource that delivers this specific depth. The target audience is employed (they have income) and desperate (they've been living in the confusion long enough to seek help). The price signals legitimacy without creating friction. The quick reference card justifies print-adjacent pricing and creates a physical artifact that reinforces ownership of the content. Users will reference this repeatedly, not once — which supports the investment framing.

## 6. Market Intel Angle

This product sits at the intersection of three massive shifts:

1. **Workplace toxicity awareness** — post-2020, employees have recalibrated their threshold for acceptable treatment. "Difficult boss" used to be a universal badge of honor. Now it's a liability signal. People are done normalizing abuse.

2. **Psychological vocabulary going mainstream** — "gaslighting," "narcissist," "DARVO" — these terms are in the room now. Employees who couldn't articulate what was happening to them five years ago now have the language. The problem isn't awareness. It's *application* — knowing when the term fits and what to do about it.

3. **The documentation economy** — people are documenting everything. Mental health, symptoms, relationships, life decisions. A structured protocol for documenting workplace manipulation fills a clear demand. This product is a documentation tool that happens to also be a survival guide.

The broader thesis: **people are no longer willing to be the only ones who remember what happened.** This product serves that infrastructure need — the reality-preservation layer that lets people trust their own experience.
