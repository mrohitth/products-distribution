## PRODUCT SKELETON: FRIENDSHIP TRAUMA RECOVERY

---

### 1. PRODUCT TITLE

**After Your Friend Gave You PTSD**

*Subtitle: A recovery roadmap for people who were damaged, not just disappointed*

---

### 2. EMOTIONAL HOOK

You know the exact moment it happened. But no one around you seems to agree on what to call it.

The panic attacks started after you stopped hanging out, not during. Your body didn't get the memo that the friendship was over — it kept bracing for the next thing. You lost friends who were mutual friends. You spiraled into Googling "can a friendship cause PTSD" at 2 a.m., half-expecting to find out you're making this up.

You're not making this up.

The hardest part isn't even the friendship — it's that you lost the ability to trust yourself. You keep second-guessing your own memory of events. You replay conversations wondering if you were the difficult one. The guilt is a constant hum underneath everything, even when the logical part of your brain knows you didn't deserve what happened.

This product doesn't ask you to "process" or "heal." It gives you a structured path out of the aftermath. By the end, you'll have rebuilt the internal architecture that person compromised — your judgment, your boundaries, and your willingness to enter rooms where people could hurt you again.

You will get your life back. Not as the person you were before, but as someone who can see clearly now, and hasn't forgotten what clarity cost.

---

### 3. PRODUCT ARCHITECTURE

---

**PART 1: NAME IT SO YOU CAN LEAVE IT**

---

**Chapter 1: Map the actual abuse, not the friendship you imagined**

*After reading this, you will be able to:* Create a written timeline of every pattern of harmful behavior that occurred — not a curated summary, but a specific event-by-event inventory. You'll identify at least three distinct categories of abuse (manipulation, social sabotage, boundary violation, emotional terrorism, etc.) and stop using the vague word "toxic" to describe something that had specific mechanics.

*Output: Your Abuse Pattern Map — a 2-page worksheet that separates the friendship you thought you had from the relationship you actually survived.*

---

**Chapter 2: Separate the grief from the guilt**

*After reading this, you will be able to:* Write a letter to your former friend that you will never send — but the act of writing it will reveal the difference between mourning the person you believed existed and mourning the person who actually exists. You will correctly identify which emotions are grief (valid) and which are guilt (not yours to carry), and you will stop using guilt to postpone your recovery.

*Output: The Grief/Guilt Triage Sheet — a guided inventory with three columns: what you miss, why it wasn't real, what's left to feel.*

---

**Chapter 3: Name your trauma response without minimizing it**

*After reading this, you will be able to:* Identify and categorize your specific PTSD symptoms using a clinically grounded but non-pathologizing framework. You will know whether you are experiencing acute stress responses, complex PTSD markers, or attachment disruption — and more importantly, you will stop treating your symptoms as proof that you're weak. You will know when what you're experiencing is actually common among survivors of interpersonal trauma.

*Output: Your Trauma Response Profile — a self-assessment framework with four categories (physiological, cognitive, emotional, relational) and specific action steps for each.*

---

**PART 2: GET YOURSELF BACK**

---

**Chapter 4: Rewrite the story you keep telling yourself**

*After reading this, you will be able to:* Identify the three core false narratives your nervous system adopted during the abusive friendship — things like "my needs are unreasonable," "I'm lucky to have people who tolerate me," or "people leave when they see the real me." You will rewrite each one with an accurate counter-statement backed by the evidence from Chapter 1, and you will practice the rewritten version until it doesn't feel like a lie.

*Output: The Narrative Revision Worksheet — three pre-written false beliefs with fill-in-the-blank rewrites and a 7-day reinforcement script.*

---

**Chapter 5: Reclaim the internal authority they took from you**

*After reading this, you will be able to:* Make three small decisions right now — alone, without consulting anyone, without second-guessing — and document the fact that you made them. You will practice re-establishing your own approval as valid. You will identify the specific moment you started looking outside yourself for permission, and you will build a daily checkpoint system that prevents you from outsourcing your judgment.

*Output: Your Authority Restoration Log — a 5-entry log with prompts that force decision-making without external validation.*

---

**Chapter 6: Return to the world without going numb**

*After reading this, you will be able to:* Attend one social event where your former friend's name might come up, with a specific three-step plan for managing the conversation. You will have practiced the exact phrases to use when someone brings them up casually, and you will have already determined which friends you need to brief and which relationships are salvageable. You will not avoid the event or white-knuckle through it — you will move through it with a plan.

*Output: The Re-Entry Script Kit — four scripts for common scenarios (casual mention, direct question, mutual friend's guilt trip, "but they're sorry now").*

---

**PART 3: BECOME UNCOMPROMISABLE**

---

**Chapter 7: Build a boundary you would actually enforce**

*After reading this, you will be able to:* Draft your first non-negotiable boundary — not a polite request or a soft preference, but a line with a consequence attached. You will practice stating it in your own voice (not the version your former friend would have approved of). You will identify why past boundaries failed: they were stated to the wrong person, without enforcement, or without recognizing the patterns that made you drop them in the first place.

*Output: Your Boundary Blueprint — a structured template that forces you to name the boundary, the consequence, and the specific moment you will enforce it.*

---

**Chapter 8: Stop making yourself small to stay safe**

*After reading this, you will be able to:* Identify the three ways you have been making yourself smaller in other relationships since the friendship ended — over-apologizing, performing niceness, preemptively managing someone else's comfort. You will reverse each one with a specific replacement behavior and practice it in your lowest-stakes relationship first. You will stop using "setting boundaries" as a way to withdraw rather than to connect more honestly.

*Output: The Shrinking Reversal Guide — a three-column worksheet: old behavior, trigger context, new response.*

---
