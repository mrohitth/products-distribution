# Product Skeleton: Emergency Contractor Damage Control

---

## 1. Product Title

**Contractor Crisis Playbook** *(27 characters)*

*Alternative options:*

- *Stop Contractor Bleeding* (25 characters)
- *Renovation Emergency Guide* (28 characters)
- *When Your Contractor Fails* (26 characters)

**Title rationale:** "Playbook" signals tactical, repeatable solutions—not abstract advice. "Crisis" immediately qualifies the audience: you already have a problem. No clickbait, no fluff. The title is a filter that attracts high-intent buyers mid-disaster.

---

## 2. Emotional Hook

*(200 words)*

You're four months into a renovation that should have taken eight weeks. Your contractor keeps asking for more money. The work is substandard. He blames other trades, supply chain delays, and your "unrealistic expectations." Meanwhile, your bank account is bleeding and your family is living in a construction zone.

You've probably already googled "what to do about a bad contractor" and found generic advice about checking credentials and reading reviews—as if you hadn't done all that before hiring him.

Here's the thing nobody tells you: you have legal and financial weapons right now. Stop-payment notices. Preliminary liens. Back charge rights. Scope freezes. Most homeowners don't know these tools exist until it's too late—or they learn about them the hard way, after they've already paid out another $15,000 they can't recover.

This guide is for the moment you realize your contractor is not going to fix things—that you're past the point of hoping for good faith, and you need a plan to stop the damage and recover what you can.

You are not helpless. You are not stuck. There is a path forward, and it starts with knowing exactly what leverage you actually have.

## 3. Product Architecture

### Part 1: Damage Assessment & Evidence Lockdown

*Before you make any moves, you need to know exactly where you stand.*

---

**Chapter 1: Conduct a Line-by-Line Scope Audit**

*After reading this chapter, you will be able to:* Create a documented comparison of exactly what work was contracted versus what has been completed, with dates and change orders—if any exist—organized in a single reference document.

*Concrete output:* A scope audit worksheet with columns for contracted scope, completed work, and discrepancies, ready to present to an attorney or use in negotiations.

---

**Chapter 2: Secure All Documentation in Legally Admissible Format**

*After reading this chapter, you will be able to:* Identify which of your communications, photos, contracts, and invoices meet evidentiary standards, and organize them in a format that holds up if you need to file a complaint or pursue legal action.

*Concrete output:* A documentation checklist with timestamps, photo metadata preservation steps, and a secure storage protocol for texts, emails, and written correspondence.

---

**Chapter 3: Calculate Your Total Financial Exposure**

*After reading this chapter, you will be able to:* Determine the exact dollar amount of money already paid versus work completed, projected costs to fix defects, and estimated costs to complete the project with a new contractor—so you know your walk-away number and your negotiation floor.

*Concrete output:* A financial exposure spreadsheet that separates recoverable funds from sunk costs, with a section for projected remediation costs.

---

### Part 2: Legal & Financial Weapons

*The tools that shift leverage back to you.*

---

**Chapter 4: Issue a Stop-Work Scope Freeze**

*After reading this chapter, you will be able to:* Draft and send a legally sound scope freeze letter that formally stops additional work and additional payment obligations, buying you time to assess your options without further exposure.

*Concrete output:* A scope freeze letter template tailored for your state/jurisdiction's requirements, with notes on delivery methods that create legal proof of receipt.

---

**Chapter 5: File Preliminary Liens and Stop-Payment Notices**

*After reading this chapter, you will be able to:* Understand the difference between a mechanic's lien (which you may be able to file against your own property) and a stop-payment notice (which interrupts funds flowing to your contractor from a lender), and know the exact deadlines in your state.

*Concrete output:* A jurisdiction-specific timeline chart showing statutory deadlines for lien filings and stop-payment notices, with a step-by-step filing checklist.

---

**Chapter 6: Understand Back Charges and Setoff Rights**

*After reading this chapter, you will be able to:* Identify whether your contract contains back charge provisions, and calculate the amounts you can legitimately withhold or "charge back" to your contractor for incomplete work, defects, or damages to other trades.

*Concrete output:* A back charge calculation worksheet that maps your contractual rights to specific dollar amounts you can recover from future payments owed.

---

### Part 3: Contractor Communication & Escalation

*How to talk to someone who's already shown you who they are.*

---

**Chapter 7: Send the Freeze Letter Without Starting a War**

*After reading this chapter, you will be able to:* Deliver a scope freeze communication that is firm, legally protective, and doesn't trigger a defensive escalation—framing it as a business decision, not a personal attack.

*Concrete output:* A communication script that includes exactly what to say (and what to never say) in a freeze letter, plus guidance on calls and in-person conversations.

---

**Chapter 8: Know When to Bring in an Attorney—and What to Ask For**

*After reading this chapter, you will be able to:* Evaluate whether your situation warrants legal counsel based on dollar exposure and complexity, find a construction litigation attorney without overpaying, and give them exactly what they need for an initial consultation.

*Concrete output:* A lawyer briefing packet template: the document package you bring to an attorney on day one so you're not paying for their time to organize your paperwork.

---

**Chapter 9: Evaluate Settlement vs. Litigation with Clear Eyes**

*After reading this chapter, you will be able to:* Assess the true cost of litigation versus settlement—including time, legal fees, stress, and recovery likelihood—so you make this decision based on math, not emotion.

*Concrete output:* A settlement vs. litigation decision matrix that weighs financial recovery, time investment, and strategic outcomes for your specific situation.

---

### Part 4: Recovery & Next Project

*Closing this chapter and building something better.*

---

**Chapter 10: Pursue Alternative Dispute Resolution Without Giving Up Your Rights**

*After reading this chapter, you will be able to:* File for mediation or arbitration if your contract includes those provisions, or negotiate a binding settlement agreement that closes the matter permanently—without court costs.

*Concrete output:* A pre-mediation preparation checklist that organizes your evidence, establishes your walk-away position, and structures a settlement term sheet.

---

**Chapter 11: Find and Vet a Replacement Contractor Without Repeating Mistakes**

*After reading this chapter, you will be able to:* Run a background check that verifies licensing, insurance, litigation history, and customer references—and structure an interview process that surfaces warning signs before you sign another contract.

*Concrete output:* A vetting checklist and reference call script that includes three questions most homeowners don't think to ask (but experienced ones always do).

---

**Chapter 12: Structure Future Projects with Payment Protections Built In**

*After reading this chapter, you will be able to:* Negotiate a payment schedule that aligns money with milestones, includes specific defect correction periods, and contains the clauses most likely to protect you if things go wrong—without pricing yourself out of qualified contractors.

*Concrete output:* A contract clause checklist with red-line language you can propose, plus a milestone payment schedule template tied to specific deliverables.

---

- A story about a homeowner who filed a stop-payment notice at the right time and recovered $30,000 that would have otherwise vanished—specific about the mechanics, not just "they got their money back"
