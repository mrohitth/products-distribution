## 2. EMOTIONAL HOOK

**You did the math. Your stipend should cover everything. Then tax season hit.**

The first year you filed taxes on fellowship or stipend income, you probably did one of two things: either you owed more than you expected and scrambled to cover it, or you spent the entire spring convinced you were doing it wrong and didn't have anyone to ask. That's not a you problem. That's a system problem.

Fellowships and stipends are taxed differently than any income most people have encountered before. Your university doesn't automatically withhold federal income tax or Social Security tax from your stipend in the way a normal paycheck works. Your funding letter doesn't say how much of your award is taxable. Your department's "tax info" sheet is a PDF from 2019. And nobody in your cohort fully understands it either — they're just guessing together and hoping for the best.

**"What's Left"** is the guide that exists because nobody explained this clearly before you needed to know it. By the time you finish this, you'll know exactly what portion of your stipend counts as taxable income, how federal and state brackets apply to your specific situation, what estimated payments you're responsible for, and which deductions grad students actually qualify for — not the generic ones, the real ones that apply when you're living on $28,000/year.

You will stop guessing. You will stop being short.

## 3. PRODUCT ARCHITECTURE

### PART 1: "What They Actually Gave You" — Decoding Your Funding Letter

*The income types grad students encounter — and why the difference matters to the IRS.*

**Chapter 1:** Identify which of your income streams is a fellowship, a stipend, an RA/TA salary, or a training grant — and what each one means for your tax forms. *You'll be able to categorize every line of your funding letter and know which IRS form category applies to each.*

**Chapter 2:** Find your 1098-T and understand what it does and doesn't tell you about your taxable income. *You'll locate your tuition statement, understand why your university issues it, and recognize that it does not make your stipend income tax-free.*

**Chapter 3:** Determine whether your stipend is subject to FICA (Social Security/Medicare) withholding at your university — and why some grad students owe thousands in back taxes because no one told them. *You'll know whether your employer withheld FICA or whether you're responsible for self-employment tax on a portion of your income.*

---

### PART 2: "What the Government Actually Wants" — Federal Tax Structure

*The actual brackets, the actual math, the actual number you owe.*

**Chapter 4:** Calculate your taxable stipend income using the fellowship exclusion and understand what counts as qualified education expenses for that deduction. *You'll compute your actual taxable stipend figure — not the full number on your funding letter — using the real IRS formula, not an online estimate.*

**Chapter 5:** Apply federal tax brackets to your specific income level, accounting for the standard deduction and any QBI (qualified business income) considerations for RA/TA work. *You'll know your exact federal tax liability within a few hundred dollars before you file, so there are no surprises.*

**Chapter 6:** Understand quarterly estimated tax payments — who owes them, how to calculate them, and the penalty threshold. *You'll know whether you need to make quarterly payments, how to set them up in under an hour on the IRS website, and what the penalty threshold actually is so you're not caught off guard.*

---

### PART 3: "The State Problem" — Where Your Fellowship Gets Taxed

*Why "state tax" is the most underestimated line item in a grad student's budget — and how to know exactly what you'll owe.*

**Chapter 7:** Determine your state tax residency status and what "domicile vs. resident" means for students who moved across state lines for graduate school. *You'll know whether you're filing as a full-year resident, part-year resident, or nonresident in your current state — and what that means for what you owe.*

**Chapter 8:** Navigate state-specific fellowship taxation rules, with focused breakdowns for the top 5 grad school states (CA, NY, TX, IL, MA). *You'll pull your state's specific treatment of fellowship income from the guide, determine whether your state taxes fellowships at all, and find your state tax bracket range in under 10 minutes.*

**Chapter 9:** Calculate your combined federal + state tax liability on your stipend income and build a back-of-envelope withholding plan so you're not short next year. *You'll produce a two-line calculation — your estimated federal tax and your estimated state tax — that lets you plan your monthly net accurately before your next stipend payment arrives.*

---

### PART 4: "What You Can Actually Keep" — Deductions, Credits & Staying Clean

*The specific deductions grad students qualify for — and the mistakes that trigger audits or penalties.*

**Chapter 10:** Identify which grad student expenses are deductible as "necessary and ordinary" education expenses when associated with RA/TA employment (and which aren't). *You'll have a working list of deductible categories — home office, supplies, required software, professional development — with the IRS language that makes each one defensible.*

**Chapter 11:** Understand the student loan interest deduction, the tuition and fees deduction, and the Lifetime Learning Credit as they apply to grad students with modest incomes. *You'll determine whether you qualify for any education credits or deductions, what the income phase-out thresholds are, and how to claim them correctly on your 1040.*

**Chapter 12:** Execute a year-end tax checklist built for the academic calendar — what to track from September through April, what receipts to keep, what your department's payroll office can confirm for you. *You'll have a one-page checklist you can print and tape to your desk that tells you exactly what to do each quarter and by what date, aligned to the academic year not the calendar year.*

## 4. KEY HUMAN TOUCH ELEMENTS

**The Fellowship Math Walkthrough (Part 1, Chapter 2):** Walk through a real worked example — a $32,000 stipend at a large state university, with $8,000 in qualified tuition and fees. Show exactly how the fellowship exclusion calculation works step by step, showing the reader both the answer and the reasoning. This is the single most-prized piece of content in this space — a worked example that actually matches their numbers.

**The "What State Are You?" Decision Tree (Part 3, Chapter 7):** Use a simple flow-chart-style format: "Did you move from another state? → Were you a resident before? → Did you keep a lease/vote registration/driver's license in your home state?" Guide them to their correct residency status with minimal jargon. This is where the "I was short the first year because I underestimated state tax" pain is directly addressed.

**The Quarterly Payment Calculator Template (Part 2, Chapter 6):** Provide a pre-filled spreadsheet that takes their stipend amount, applies the fellowship exclusion, produces a federal estimate and a state estimate, and gives them the quarterly payment amounts. Students want to plug in their number and have an answer. Give them that.

**The "Why Your 1098-T Doesn't Mean What You Think" Callout Box (Part 1, Chapter 2):** This is a recurring misconception — students see the 1098-T and assume their tuition payment makes their stipend tax-free. Clear, direct correction with the actual rule. No hedging.

## 6. MARKET INTEL ANGEL

**Thesis:** Graduate student stipends represent a growing, structurally overlooked income category as federal research funding increases and more universities move toward fellowship-based funding models (particularly in STEM and humanities PhD programs). These students have real, taxed income — often $25K–$45K annually — but receive no structured tax education. The financial aid office says "we can't give tax advice." The department says "ask your advisor." Students are left to learn from Reddit threads and anxiety.

This is a pure information asymmetry product. The knowledge gap is real, the stakes are measurable (underpayment penalties, back taxes), and the audience is large and recurrent — new cohorts of first-year grad students arrive every September with the exact same problem. The product is evergreen by structure: tax law changes, but the fellowship/stipend income category and the confusion around it does not. A yearly update cycle keeps the product fresh and the email list engaged. The state-specific component creates a natural expansion path (all 50 states, sold individually or as a bundle).

**Positioning:** This does not compete with TurboTax or H&R Block. It competes with "I'll figure it out myself" — and wins because the alternative is genuinely painful, and the guide costs less than a single hour with a tax professional who doesn't know the fellowship rules either.
