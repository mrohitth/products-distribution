
# PRODUCT SKELETON

**Don't Get Fleeced: The Contractor Vetting System**

---

## EMOTIONAL HOOK

You don't have to be naive to get played. You just have to hire the wrong person once.

If you've ever watched a contractor pad an invoice, claim something was "out of scope," or leave you owing money you never agreed to pay—you know the specific violation of it. Not just the money (though that's bad enough). It's the feeling of being a mark in your own home. The helplessness of arguing with someone who does this for a living while you've got a job and kids and a hundred other things.

Here's the thing nobody tells you: even contractors who do good work will test boundaries. The industry runs on information asymmetry, and most homeowners walk in with none. You don't need to become a contractor. You need a system that makes them treat you like one.

This guide gives you that system. Not tips. Not vague advice. A complete framework—vetting checklists, contract clauses, payment structures, and red flag guides—that forces contractors to deal with you professionally or walk. By the time you're done, you'll know how to identify the reliable ones before you sign anything, protect every dollar you pay, and spot trouble while you can still do something about it.

---

## PRODUCT ARCHITECTURE

### PART 1: VETTING SYSTEM — HIRING THE RIGHT PERSON THE FIRST TIME

**Chapter 1: The Pre-Interview Scorecard**
You'll create a scoring rubric with weighted criteria (licensing, communication speed, subcontractor use, payment terms, etc.) that converts your gut feeling into a documented decision. Output: a filled scorecard for each contractor you're considering, giving you apples-to-apples comparison before emotional attachment sets in.

**Chapter 2: The 12-Question Phone Screen**
You'll ask a specific set of questions that expose how a contractor handles scope, change orders, and payment structure—and you'll know exactly which answers are red flags vs. professional norms. Output: a question list with ideal vs. concerning answers you can screenshot and reference mid-call.

**Chapter 3: The Insurance Verification Protocol**
You'll pull actual certificates of insurance, verify they're current, and understand exactly what liability coverage vs. workers' comp actually protects. Output: a three-step verification checklist and a template email you can send to their insurer directly.

---

### PART 2: CONTRACT ARCHITECTURE — BUILDING A DOCUMENT THAT PROTECTS

**Chapter 4: The 9 Non-Negotiable Clause Modifications**
You'll identify the specific terms in a contractor's standard agreement that expose you to scope creep and surprise charges—and you'll know exactly what to add or change. Output: a marked-up sample contract with annotations explaining each modification.

**Chapter 5: The Itemized Scope of Work Appendix**
You'll build a scope document that breaks the project into granular line items (materials, labor, timeline, permitting, cleanup), forcing ambiguity out of the project before it starts. Output: a fill-in scope template with 40+ line items covering typical renovation categories.

**Chapter 6: The Change Order Gatekeeper Procedure**
You'll establish a written protocol requiring any scope change to be signed and priced before work continues—no verbal agreements, no "we'll figure it out later." Output: a change order form template and a script for responding when a contractor claims something is out of scope mid-project.

---

### PART 3: PAYMENT PROTECTION — YOUR MONEY STAYS YOURS

**Chapter 7: The Reverse Escrow Payment Schedule**
You'll structure payments as deliverables at defined milestones rather than upfront deposits, reversing the industry's power dynamic so you hold leverage until work is complete. Output: a payment schedule template that front-loads protection (e.g., 10% initial, 30% at framing, 40% at rough-in, 20% at punch list completion) with specific holdback language.

**Chapter 8: The Lien Waiver Documentation Chain**
You'll require lien waivers at each payment stage, creating a paper trail that protects your property title from mechanic's liens even if the contractor disappears or has disputes with suppliers. Output: a lien waiver collection tracker with state-specific requirements for your jurisdiction.

**Chapter 9: The Final Payment Negotiation Framework**
You'll know exactly when and how to release the final payment (hint: after punch list completion, not before), and you'll have a documented process for handling incomplete items without getting locked into a hold that goes nowhere. Output: a final payment protocol with email templates that document the completion checklist and timeline.

---

### PART 4: RED FLAG INTELLIGENCE — KNOWING WHEN TO EXIT

**Chapter 10: The First Week Warning Sign Scanner**
You'll identify the behavioral and procedural red flags that appear in the first days of a project—pressure to start before contracts are signed, reluctance to provide references, vague answers on timeline—while you still have the power to stop. Output: a one-page "week one abort criteria" checklist you can reference on day two or three.

**Chapter 11: The Mid-Project Deterioration Index**
You'll recognize the pattern of slow erosion—small scope creep, payment requests accelerating faster than completion, communication drying up—that typically precedes serious problems, and you'll know the intervention threshold. Output: a deterioration scorecard with escalation triggers and exact responses at each stage.

**Chapter 12: The Emergency Exit Procedure**
You'll have a documented process for terminating a contractor relationship with minimal financial exposure and maximum legal standing, including proper documentation to protect yourself if you end up in dispute. Output: a termination letter template, a dispute documentation checklist, and a step-by-step process for securing your property and remaining funds.

## KEY HUMAN TOUCH ELEMENTS

**The "Even Good Ones Test You" Framing**
Open with a story (vetted, anonymized) of a homeowner who hired a well-reviewed contractor, paid a 40% deposit, and found out two weeks in that every single material choice was going to cost extra because it wasn't "in the original scope"—even though it had never been discussed. Use this to normalize that this isn't about bad luck; it's about a system the industry runs on.

**The "You're Not Paranoid" Validation**
Include a sidebar or pull-quote section: "If something feels off, it's because something IS off. Trust the pattern, not the individual." Homeowners who get burned often second-guess themselves afterward. This guide gives them permission to trust their instincts earlier.

**The "One Check" Check**
Frame the entire guide around a single premise: every dollar you spend on this system returns $X by preventing one common contractor dispute. Use a conservative estimate (e.g., "the average contested invoice in this category is $3,200; this guide costs less than 5% of that") to ground the purchase decision in ROI without being sleazy about it.

## MARKET INTEL ANGLE

Home improvement spending hit $567 billion in 2023, with professional labor as the fastest-growing cost category. As materials have commoditized, craftsmanship and project management have become the variable—and the point of leverage. Homeowners are increasingly navigating complex projects (ADUs, full renos, solar installations) that require contractors at a scale they've never managed before.

Simultaneously, contractor licensing and complaint databases exist but aren't consumer-friendly, and contractor reviews on platforms like Houzz and Angi are gaming-optimized, not information-optimized. There's a massive gap between "this contractor is well-reviewed" and "this contractor will handle your $80,000 addition professionally."

This guide sits at the intersection of financial protection (like legal document products) and consumer empowerment (like home inspection guides). The audience is large, the pain is specific, and the solution is concrete. This is a "keep in your filing cabinet" product that homeowners reference every time a project comes up—repeat usage across decades of home ownership.
