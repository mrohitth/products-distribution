# Product Skeleton

**Title:** Tax Checklist for Business Owners

---

## 1. Emotional Hook

Tax season is that one time of year where your business knowledge suddenly feels incomplete. You know you made money. You know you spent money. You know you're supposed to do *something* with the IRS. But right about now, you're staring at a screen asking yourself: *Which form do I even start with?*

That's not a knowledge problem. That's a navigation problem. And it's exactly why most small business owners delay filing, miss deductions, or worse — file wrong and face corrections six months later.

This guide solves the navigation problem. No accounting jargon. No "talk to your CPA" deferrals. Just: here's your entity type, here's every form you need, here's the exact order to complete them, and here's what each line means in plain English.

By the time you finish this guide, you'll walk into tax season with a completed checklist, zero ambiguity about which forms to file, and the confidence to sign your name on every submission.

## 2. Product Architecture

**Part 1: Know Your Entity Before You File (Chapters 1–3)**

- **Chapter 1:** Identify your exact business entity type from five categories — sole proprietor, single-member LLC, multi-member LLC, S-Corp, or partnership — using a decision-tree checklist with yes/no questions. You will leave this chapter with a labeled entity type you can state on every form.
- **Chapter 2:** Map your business income and expense categories to the correct IRS form — 1040 Schedule C, 1065, 1120-S, or 1120 — with a side-by-side comparison table showing which forms apply to which entities. You will leave this chapter knowing which single form stack applies to your situation and why.
- **Chapter 3:** Identify every mandatory federal filing deadline for your entity type, including quarterly estimated tax deadlines, extension deadlines, and state filing windows — and learn how to set up a calendar system that flags each one 14 days in advance. You will leave this chapter with a complete, personalized tax calendar you can keep on your desk all year.

**Part 2: Entity-Specific Filing Playbooks (Chapters 4–9)**

- **Chapter 4 (Sole Proprietor):** Complete a Schedule C from scratch — from gathering your profit/loss records through calculating net profit and attaching it to your 1040 — with a line-by-line annotation showing exactly where to enter income, write off home office expenses, and claim the standard mileage deduction. You will leave this chapter with a completed Schedule C ready to file or hand to a preparer.
- **Chapter 5 (Sole Proprietor):** Calculate your self-employment tax using Schedule SE, learn which half of the SE tax is deductible, and verify your quarterly estimated tax payments using Form 1040-ES. You will leave this chapter with your SE tax number in hand and a decision on whether your quarterly payments are on track or overdue.
- **Chapter 6 (Single-Member LLC):** File your LLC's "disregarded entity" status correctly — understanding when to file just a 1040 with Schedule C versus when to file separately — and learn how to open a business bank account without triggering unexpected tax obligations. You will leave this chapter knowing exactly what your LLC does and does not require the IRS to see.
- **Chapter 7 (Multi-Member LLC):** File Form 1065 for your partnership LLC — understanding the difference between partner distributions, guaranteed payments, and ordinary income — and complete a Schedule K-1 for each member with your own K-1. You will leave this chapter with a completed Form 1065 package ready for submission.
- **Chapter 8 (S-Corp):** File Form 1120-S correctly — including the process of paying yourself a "reasonable salary" as an officer and distributing remaining income as distributions — and understand why an S-Corp can legitimately lower your self-employment tax. You will leave this chapter with a completed S-Corp filing package and a clear salary-to-distribution ratio reviewed annually.
- **Chapter 9 (S-Corp):** Complete Form 941 quarterly for payroll tax filings and understand the relationship between your S-Corp salary, payroll tax obligations, and your annual 1120-S filing. You will leave this chapter with your quarterly 941 schedule confirmed and an understanding of which payroll events trigger new filings.

**Part 3: What You Can Legally Reduce (Chapters 10–12)**

- **Chapter 10:** Capture every deductible business expense you're currently overlooking — including home office deductions, business vehicle write-offs (standard mileage vs. actual expense method), equipment purchases under Section 179, and health insurance premiums for self-employed owners. You will leave this chapter with a deduction inventory list tailored to your entity type, with specific dollar thresholds and documentation requirements for each.
- **Chapter 11:** Build a year-round expense tracking workflow using either a spreadsheet system or a low-cost app, organized by IRS expense categories so your records are filing-ready year-round — not a nightmare in April. You will leave this chapter with a 12-month expense tracking template and a category structure the IRS will recognize if audited.
- **Chapter 12:** Make quarterly estimated tax payments without guessing — using your prior year tax return to calculate current year obligations, understanding the safe harbor threshold, and learning what to do if you underpaid in Q3. You will leave this chapter with a four-quarter estimated tax payment schedule that adjusts automatically based on your actual income fluctuations.

**Part 4: Deadlines, Extensions, and What Comes Next (Chapters 13–15)**

- **Chapter 13:** File a valid tax extension (Form 4868 or 7004) correctly — understanding that an extension gives you more time to FILE but not more time to OWE — and learn how to estimate your remaining tax liability to send a payment with your extension. You will leave this chapter with an extension filed properly or a confirmed April deadline you're not missing.
- **Chapter 14:** Handle multi-state filing obligations if your business operates in more than one state — understanding economic nexus thresholds, state-level filing requirements, and how to file a composite return if you have partners in different states. You will leave this chapter with a list of every state where you have a filing obligation and the forms required in each.
- **Chapter 15:** Organize your business records for a three-year IRS audit window — learning exactly which documents to keep, which can be stored digitally, and what a "reasonable basis" for a deduction looks like in practice. You will leave this chapter with an annual record-keeping checklist you can execute every January like a system.

## 3. Key Human Touch Elements

- **The "Two AM Panic" opener** (used in Part 1): Every small business owner has had the moment where they can't sleep because they're not sure if they filed the right thing. Name it. Normalize it. Then give them the fix immediately.
- **The Freelancer and the LLC story** (Chapter 6): A freelance developer who formed an LLC thinking it would "save them money on taxes," then discovered they filed nothing differently that year because they didn't understand the filing mechanics. Real, relatable, and instructive.
- **The S-Corp Salary Surprise** (Chapter 8): A small business owner who converted to S-Corp, underpaid themselves a salary, and received an IRS notice — this becomes a teaching moment about reasonable compensation requirements without being preachy.
- **The Audit Window framing** (Chapter 15): Three years sounds like a long time until you realize that every year you haven't organized records, you're one missed document away from a defended position versus a closed case.

## 4. Format & Pricing

**Format:** PDF + 15 printable checklists (one per chapter's core output), delivered as a single download with a master filing checklist as a front matter reference sheet.

- PDF for reading and reference
- Individual checklist PDFs for printing and physically checking off during filing sessions
- Designed for screen-first AND desk-print use — most small business owners will want something tangible to mark through during their filing session

**Pricing Rationale:** $27. This sits in the "low-stakes decision, high-value outcome" sweet spot for small business owners. They spend more than that on a single hour with a CPA. This product makes their CPA meeting more productive — or removes the need for one entirely on straightforward filings. The printable checklists alone justify this price as a practical tool, not just information.

## 5. Market Intel Angle

This product sits at the intersection of two infrastructure shifts: the explosion of self-employment and gig economy income (70+ million Americans file some form of self-employment income), and the growing distrust of "just hire an accountant" advice when you're running lean. There's a genuine market of small business owners who don't have complex tax situations — they just don't know which forms to touch and in what order.

As the independent economy expands, the pain point isn't "I can't afford an accountant" — it's "I don't know what to ask my accountant, and I'm filing wrong things while paying them." This guide solves the upstream problem, and it plugs into a larger ecosystem of financial infrastructure products (bookkeeping trackers, invoice templates, quarterly review guides) that form a small business financial OS.
