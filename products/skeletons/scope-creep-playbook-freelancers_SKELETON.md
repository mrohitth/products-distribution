# Product Skeleton

## Scope Lock: The Freelancer's Boundary System

---

## 1. The Scope Lock System (20 characters)

*Stop scope creep. Get paid in full. Protect your reputation.*

---

## 2. Emotional Hook

You've been there. It's 11pm, you're finishing work that wasn't in the brief, and you're not charging for it. Maybe you stayed silent when a client said "while you're at it" for the third time this week. Maybe you've already had that conversation where you bent over backward, hoping they'd remember your goodwill later.

They won't.

Scope creep doesn't just steal your time—it erodes your rates, your confidence, and eventually your reputation. The client who pushed for "just one more revision" tells a colleague you were "difficult to work with" when you finally pushed back. The project you absorbed extra hours on? It didn't cover rent.

Here's what nobody tells you: the problem isn't that you're too nice. It's that you never had a system for saying no that felt as natural as saying yes.

**The Scope Lock System** gives you contract language you can copy today, scope frameworks that anticipate grey areas before they happen, and scripts for every boundary conversation from initial kickoff to final delivery—so you stop being a pushover by default and start being a professional by design.

## 3. Product Architecture

### Part 1: Contract Foundations

**Chapter 1: Choose Your Contract Template**
*You will BE ABLE TO DO:* Select from three contract archetypes based on your service type (deliverable-based, hourly, retainer) and know exactly which structure protects you best in each scenario.

**Chapter 2: Identify Your Non-Negotiables**
*You will BE ABLE TO DO:* List your 7 essential contract clauses (revision limits, payment milestones, kill fees, IP transfer language) and position them without apologizing in any client agreement.

**Chapter 3: Add Scope Protection Clauses**
*You will BE ABLE TO DO:* Insert a Scope Change Amendment process into any contract that creates a paper trail for additional work and ensures you get paid for it—legally sound, client-friendly language included.

---

### Part 2: Scope Architecture

**Chapter 4: Decompose Deliverables into Brick-Atomic Tasks**
*You will BE ABLE TO DO:* Break any project into granular deliverables that clients sign off on individually, eliminating the "I thought it was included" dispute at the end.

**Chapter 5: Build the Explicit Scope Boundary**
*You will BE ABLE TO DO:* Write a Scope Statement that separates in-scope from out-of-scope with enough specificity that a client reading it would know exactly what they're NOT getting.

**Chapter 6: Engineer Buffer and Revision Math**
*You will BE ABLE TO DO:* Calculate appropriate revision rounds, set realistic timelines, and price buffer time so you're never stuck absorbing scope expansion due to poor planning.

---

### Part 3: Boundary Communication Scripts

**Chapter 7: The Kickoff Redirection Script**
*You will BE ABLE TO DO:* Control the project kickoff meeting using a structured framing that establishes scope authority early and prevents "I assumed" drift before it starts.

**Chapter 8: The Grey Request Deflection**
*You will BE ABLE TO DO:* Respond to vague or out-of-scope requests ("Can you just...?" "While you're at it...") with professional language that declines without burning bridges.

**Chapter 9: The Scope Breach Confrontation**
*You will BE ABLE TO DO:* Address active scope creep with a client using a 4-step escalation script that resets expectations while preserving the relationship.

---

### Part 4: Recovery and Renegotiation

**Chapter 10: Mid-Project Scope Renegotiation**
*You will BE ABLE TO DO:* Open a renegotiation conversation when scope has legitimately shifted (client-driven change) and secure updated terms without losing the account.

**Chapter 11: The Unpaid Overage Conversation**
*You will BE ABLE TO DO:* Request payment for work already completed outside original scope using a template that removes emotion and centers documented agreement.

**Chapter 12: Exit Clauses and Professional Endings**
*You will BE ABLE TO DO:* Terminate a problematic engagement with a client using legally sound exit language that protects your reputation and remaining payments.

## 4. Key Human Touch Elements

- **The "Compliment Sandwich" Trap:** A personal anecdote about learning that diplomatic feedback actually *enabled* scope creep because clients heard the yes buried under the no. This recontextualizes directness as kindness.
- **"The Friend Who Didn't Bill":** A story about losing money on a project for someone they knew, and how the lack of contract felt like friendship but created resentment.
- **The Client Who Came Back:** A case where firm boundaries initially felt risky but resulted in a client who respected the structure and became a long-term account.
- **"Scope creep isn't a client problem, it's a system problem":** Framing that removes shame—boundary-setting isn't about being tough, it's about creating clarity everyone benefits from.

## 6. Market Intel Angle

The independent workforce isn't a trend—it's infrastructure. 70+ million Americans are freelancing, and most received zero business training. They've mastered their craft, not their commerce. As traditional employment continues to fragment, the freelancers who build operational discipline (contracts, boundaries, systems) will outlast those who don't. **Scope Lock** isn't a nice-to-have—it's the difference between freelancing as a career and freelancing as a phase.
