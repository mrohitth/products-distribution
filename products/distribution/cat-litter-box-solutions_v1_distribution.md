Title: Litter box avoidance has three root causes. Most owners only try to fix one.

Body:

I spent four months trying different litters, moving the box around, and cleaning more frequently before I understood why nothing was working.

The vet found a UTI. None of the environmental changes would have helped because the problem was medical, not behavioral.

Once that was ruled out, the environmental causes were straightforward to address:

**The five factors that matter most:**
1. One box per cat, plus one extra — not one box total
2. Uncovered box (covered boxes trap ammonia and create odor buildup that overwhelms sensitive feline noses)
3. Unscented clumping litter at 2 inches minimum depth — scented litter is a common avoidance trigger
4. Box placement away from food, loud appliances, and high-traffic areas
5. Scooped daily, fully cleaned monthly

**The stress factor:**
A new pet, a move, even a new cleaning product can trigger stress-related avoidance. Multi-cat households with shared resources (one litter box, one food bowl) frequently have tension that manifests as litter box problems.

Feliway helped in my multi-cat setup. Peer-reviewed evidence supports it for stress reduction in households with inter-cat tension.

I put together a complete diagnostic guide with all the steps. Free to download, includes a checklist you can work through in one sitting. The checklist is what I use every time I need to troubleshoot a new behavioral issue.
